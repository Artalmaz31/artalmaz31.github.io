How the ablation test works

For each of the 86 targets we take the per-position graph after compaction, rank its feature vertices by their total influence on the other feature vertices, ∑t≠v∣Bv,t∣\sum_{t \neq v} |B_{v, t}|, and keep the top 30. Each is ablated in the original FLUX.1 [schnell], at its own position: a hook subtracts its activation times its decoder vector from the MLP output there. Then the preactivation of every other vertex of the set is read again. This gives 870 ordered pairs per graph, each with an actual effect ∣Δht∣|\Delta h_t| and the effect the graph predicts, ∣Bv,t∣|B_{v, t}|. The two are in different units, so they are compared by rank and by linear correlation.

Pearson exceeds Spearman because the few high-influence pairs, which dominate a linear correlation, are predicted well, while pairs of similar low predicted influence can differ in actual effect by an order of magnitude.
